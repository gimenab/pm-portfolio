Meeting 1 • Elicitation

Foundation (sponsor)

Notes from the first meeting with the sponsor, played from a secret brief.

2026-10-20 · Video call · Simulated: Claude plays the stakeholder from a secret brief committed before the meeting.

Attendees: Laura Medina (executive director, Fundación Tabaquillar) · Gimena Fernández (PM, Quebrada Labs)

Trigger: email from Laura: "modernize how people report fires and care for wildlife in our reserves". A fire near one of the reserves last year started the idea.

What we learned

Context - The foundation runs the Red de Reservas Serranas: 3 reserves in the Córdoba hills, inside provincial protected areas. - Problems today: illegal campfires (one got out of hand two years ago), hunting with dogs in the largest reserve last year, poisoned bait on nearby farms that kills condors. - Incidents are not recorded in an orderly way: rangers share them in a WhatsApp group; serious ones are reported to the Dirección de Áreas Serranas Protegidas.

Users mentioned - Visitors (hikers, tourists, school trips) and park rangers. Reporting fire is common to both. - Rangers also need to check visitor reports in the field. - Neighbors at the foot of the hills ("they see smoke first"). - Volunteer firefighters must receive fire alerts. - University researchers have asked for condor sighting data.

Features Laura asked for (wish list, not yet prioritized) 1. "I see smoke" button with location and photo. 2. Fire-risk notices for visitors (e.g. "no fires allowed today"). 3. Public sightings map, with photos, on the foundation's WordPress site. 4. Educational species pages (tabaquillo, condor). 5. Points or badges for reporting native species. 6. Litter reports.

Constraints - Deadline: working pilot by 30 June 2027 (about 8 months). Fixed: missing it loses the grant. - Budget: USD 60,000 total; USD 12,000 already committed to a communications campaign, so USD 48,000 for the product, including the website map. - Funding: international climate fund. Requires quarterly impact reports: number of reports made with the app and response time (from report to someone arriving). - Permit: any operation inside the reserves needs approval from the Dirección de Áreas Serranas Protegidas (provincial).

Decision-makers and influence - Laura leads the project; major decisions go to the foundation's board (5 members, monthly). - Board member Raúl pushes for a donate button next to each sighting. Laura is not convinced but has not said no.

Success and baseline - Grant metrics: number of reports and response time. Foundation also wants usage and a "full, attractive" map for fundraising. - No baseline exists. Only anecdote: last year's fire took about 3 hours from a neighbor seeing smoke to firefighters arriving. - Visitors: about 18,000 last year at the largest reserve (entrance logbook), peaks in summer and winter holidays. No entry control at the other two reserves; many people enter off-trail.

Proposals made by Gimena in the meeting

  • Users must be 18+ and register with ID (DNI), for safety. Laura agreed.
  • Record people who break park rules or cause harm; reinforce control at authorized entrances. Legal implications to be checked.
  • Researchers get access to the reported data through the app/website. Laura agreed.
  • Measure a baseline before the pilot: average time from report to firefighters acting, and average visitors, to compare against app downloads and reports. Laura agreed.

Meeting 2 • Elicitation

Province + researchers

The open-data dilemma comes up here.

2026-10-23 · Video call · Simulated: Claude plays both stakeholders from secret briefs committed before the meeting.

Attendees: Marcos Ferreyra (head of area, Dirección de Áreas Serranas Protegidas) · Dr. Tomás Quiroga (ecology research group, public university) · Gimena Fernández (PM, Quebrada Labs)

Goal: permit conditions, rules for data collected inside protected areas, and what data the researchers need (open items 3, 4 and 8 from meeting 1).

What we learned

Fire reports and the official channel - Today fires are reported by phone to the provincial emergency number; the central dispatches firefighters. Rangers use radio or phone. Nothing is digital. - Red line (Marcos): fire reports from the app must reach the official emergency channel. The app adds to that channel; it does not replace calling the emergency number.

Planned provincial system (not public yet) - The province is preparing its own digital reporting system for fires and incidents in all protected areas, connected to the emergency central. Tender expected in 2027. - Marcos fears the app will overlap with it and have to be shut down. - He would support the app long term if it exports its data in an open, documented format that the future system can ingest without depending on the foundation. - No details of the tender can be shared. Who its users will be is not confirmed: do not assume it targets specialist staff only.

Rules on data inside protected areas - An internal provincial resolution makes the exact locations of condor nests and sensitive tabaquillo patches restricted. They cannot be published (risk: hunters, photographers reaching the nests).

Researchers' needs - Tomás's research grant requires publishing data in a public open repository (anyone can download, no permission). That is why he pushed for open data. - His analysis (fire recurrence by zone, condor population) needs precise data. - For a sighting to be usable in a paper it needs: species, date and time, location precision, and a "verified" flag. Without these, volume does not help. He will send the full field list. - His group offers to help verify wildlife sightings (species identification).

Recording suspicious people - Marcos: not the province's responsibility; the foundation must check it with its lawyers. The province will not endorse a foundation keeping records of citizens.

Decision: how location data is shared

Reached in the meeting, agreed by Marcos and Tomás:

Data Who sees it Condition
Aggregated, on a coarse grid (cells of about 5 km) Anyone (public map, open repository) Meets Tomás's grant; does not reveal nests (Marcos)
Precise coordinates Accredited researchers only Data agreement signed with the province

How to hide or degrade precision technically (grid, encryption, access control) to be worked out by Gimena with the tech lead.

Meeting 3 • Elicitation

Rangers + firefighters

The field users: no signal, radios, false alarms.

2026-10-25 · Video call · Simulated: Claude plays both stakeholders from secret briefs committed before the meeting.

Attendees: Sofía Arce (lead ranger, largest reserve in the Red de Reservas Serranas) · Carla Bustos (chief of a volunteer fire station; coordinates a neighbor early-warning network) · Gimena Fernández (PM, Quebrada Labs)

Goal: how fires are reported and checked today, and how app reports reach the official emergency channel (open item 1 from meeting 1, open item 3 from meeting 2).

What we learned

How fires are reported today - Rangers: VHF radio; if there is signal, also a photo to the rangers' WhatsApp group. The nearest ranger goes to check; if confirmed, they call the emergency number. - Six rangers for about 40,000 ha. No ranger on duty at night. - Firefighters get alerts two ways: the official one (someone calls the emergency number, the provincial central evaluates and activates them by radio) and the neighbor network (WhatsApp group and some neighbors with radios). The neighbor network often warns them first, so they get people and equipment ready while they wait for activation. - When nobody answers, the alert is lost or arrives late. Often the fire is first seen by satellite or from the road, when it is already large.

Who activates the firefighters - Only the provincial emergency central. Not the app, not a ranger, not a neighbor. A direct call from a ranger lets Carla get people ready, but she cannot send a crew without activation. - The central works 24 hours, but often cannot confirm a report and calls the nearest ranger to go and check.

Volunteers, no one watching screens - Firefighters are volunteers with their own jobs; nobody watches a screen at the station. They hear about fires from the central's radio or WhatsApp. - Red line (Carla): the app must not need anyone on her side watching it.

False reports - Last season a viral photo of "smoke" in the reserve was a barbecue; it took a ranger half a day to find and rule out. - Sofía wants visitor reports checked by a ranger before they reach the firefighters. - Carla's concern: a report waiting for a ranger (at 3 am, or when the nearest one is two hours away) loses the time the app was meant to save.

Use what they already have - Both want the app to send alerts into the channels they already use (WhatsApp groups, radio), not replace them. Carla: "if everyone has to move to something new, it falls apart in two weeks." - Rangers watch their WhatsApp group all day.

Field conditions (Sofía) - Must work without signal: reports are stored and sent when signal returns. - One hand, gloves on, low battery use. Photo with location plus a voice message is the fastest way. - Must be faster than sending a WhatsApp message, or rangers will not use it. - No extra paperwork: no form to fill after each report. - No new device to carry or charge (in reply to the satellite idea; cost also unclear).

What a report needs (Carla) - Exact location, a photo, wind direction if possible. - Voice must not replace location: "smoke behind the hill" does not say which hill.

Protocols - The central's protocol decides how reports are classified and who is sent. The fire station has an internal protocol for how they respond; Carla will send it. - Rangers have no written protocol; they decide by experience (smoke color, wind, distance to houses). Sofía can explain it. - Carla: a low / medium / high level helps her prepare, but no report marked "low" may be held back or hidden.

Agreed in the meeting

  • An app report (photo + coordinates + text or voice) reaches the emergency central as one more report, like a phone call. The central decides whether to act.
  • The sender gets a confirmation that the report was sent.
  • Reports made without signal are stored and sent automatically when signal returns.

Not resolved: who sees a visitor report first

Stakeholder Position

Carla (firefighters) No report may wait for someone to look at it; at night no one does.

To be worked out in analysis (for example, the report goes to the central and to the rangers' group at the same time, and a ranger's confirmation raises its priority). Needs checking with the three of them.

Purpose

Today a fire is reported by phone or radio, and if nobody answers the alert is lost or arrives late; often the fire is first seen by satellite or from the road, when it is already large (M3). In 2025, 62% of fires in Córdoba were under 10 ha, and most fires are human-caused (gap analysis, IDECOR and CONAE). The project builds a pilot app so that whoever sees smoke first (neighbors, visitors, rangers) can report it with an exact location and a photo, also without signal, and the report reaches the official emergency channel. It also records wildlife sightings for research, and measures the number of reports and the response time the grant requires (M1, M2, M3; summarized, inferred).
